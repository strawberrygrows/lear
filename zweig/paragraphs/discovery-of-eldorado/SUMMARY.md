# *The Discovery of Eldorado* (*Die Entdeckung Eldorados*)

**Author:** Stefan Zweig (1881–1942)
**Collection:** *Sternstunden der Menschheit*. First published 1927 (Insel-Bücherei 165, Leipzig) as one of five miniatures; later in the enlarged editions.
**Source text:** S. Fischer Verlag, 1951, as transcribed by Projekt Gutenberg-DE ([chapter 9](https://projekt-gutenberg.org/authors/stefan-zweig/books/sternstunden-der-menschheit/chapter/9/)). Cleaned copy in `source/die-entdeckung-eldorados.txt`.
**Length:** 24 paragraphs in 7 sections, about 2,400 words of German.
**Status:** all paragraphs `claude-drafted`; `translation_final` empty.

## What it is

The rise and ruin of Johann August Suter: the Swiss bankrupt who founds New Helvetia in the Sacramento valley, finds gold on his land in January 1848, is ruined by the rush, wins his lawsuit, and is ruined again by the verdict. Zweig builds it on a refrain, ‘the richest man in the world’, which he reverses three times. His source, which he names in the last sentence, is Blaise Cendrars’s novel *L’Or* (1925).

## For editorial attention (in priority order)

1. **Title of the collection.** *Sternstunden der Menschheit* has been published in English as *The Tide of Fortune* (Eden and Cedar Paul, 1940), *Decisive Moments in History* (1999) and *Shooting Stars* (Anthea Bell, 2013). All three translations are still in copyright and were not consulted. The page currently gives the German title only. A new English title is your call.
2. **Period terms (p01, p05).** *Rothäute* rendered ‘Red Indians’, *Farbigen* ‘coloured men’ (DECISION-Z07). These are the most likely places for you to want a different choice.
3. **The comma-chains.** Zweig’s long comma-spliced sentences (p01, p11, p14, p21, p23) are kept with very little repunctuation (DECISION-Z02). Read these aloud. If any sentence stalls, a semicolon is the permitted remedy; a dash is not.
4. **‘Suter’, not ‘Sutter’** (p01, DECISION-Z03). Kept as Zweig wrote it.
5. **The *Recht* chain (p22–p24).** ‘His right’, ‘his rights’, ‘no justice has been done’, ‘the one right of a great destiny’. English cannot use one word throughout; check the final sentence still lands.
6. **Historical departures** (p21, p23) are noted but not corrected (DECISION-Z05). A short introduction could deal with them instead of the notes, if you prefer a cleaner page.
7. **Source edition.** The 1951 Fischer text may contain Zweig’s later revisions to the 1927 text. For US publication, collating against the 1927 Insel text would be prudent (see README, Rights).
