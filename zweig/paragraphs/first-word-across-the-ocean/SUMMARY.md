**Author:** Stefan Zweig (1881–1942)
**Collection:** *Sternstunden der Menschheit*. Not, as far as I can tell, in the 1927 first edition; probably first published in the enlarged edition of 1943 (Stockholm: Bermann-Fischer). **To be verified**: this decides the US copyright position (see README, Rights).
**Source text:** S. Fischer Verlag, 1951, via Projekt Gutenberg-DE ([chapter 11](https://projekt-gutenberg.org/authors/stefan-zweig/books/sternstunden-der-menschheit/chapter/11/)), as pasted by the editor. Cleaned copy in `source/das-erste-wort-ueber-den-ozean.txt`.
**Length:** 32 paragraphs in 9 sections, about 5,600 words of German.
**Status:** all paragraphs `claude-drafted`; `translation_final` empty.

## What it is

Cyrus W. Field and the Atlantic telegraph: the failed voyages of 1857 and 1858, the cable that works for a few weeks in August 1858 and then dies in the middle of New York's celebrations, and the success of the *Great Eastern* in 1866. Zweig lays the Passion over it (‘The Great Hosanna’, ‘The Great *Crucifige*’, Field ‘risen again’) and ends with a warning about humanity turning its own means of union to its destruction.

## For editorial attention (in priority order)

1. **US copyright (see above).** If this story first appeared in 1943, it should not go on a US-facing public site until 2039, or only with that risk accepted.
2. **The Valentia prayer (p12), the one `query`.** Spoken in English in 1857; Zweig gives it in German. Fresh translation in Authorised Version idiom until the original is found in a contemporary report.
3. **Section title ‘The Great *Crucifige*’.** Latin kept, as a foreign fragment. ‘The Great Crucify Him’ is the alternative.
4. **One periodic sentence recast (p25).** ‘The longed-for news comes too late…’; Zweig holds the subject to the end. Noted.
5. **Historical slips kept** (DECISION-Z05): dynamos in the 1850s (p06, p30), the Telegraph Construction and Maintenance Company in 1856 (p08), four funnels on the *Great Eastern* (p31), the 1858 cable ‘found’ in 1866 (p32). One slip corrected: ‘the Civil War between North and South *America*’ (p29).
6. **Field’s crossings:** thirty-one (p07) and ‘the thirtieth time’ (p31). Zweig’s inconsistency, kept.
